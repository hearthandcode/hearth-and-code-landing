# AI Literacy page · claim and source map

This map supports review of the local AI Literacy page. It records what each substantive passage is, which sources support it, and where the page presents Hearth & Code guidance or interpretation rather than an externally established fact. It does not approve the text or authorize republication.

| S7 | [Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, consolidated text as of 27 July 2026](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A02024R1689-20260727) · current consolidated legal text | Amended Article 4(1) requires providers and deployers to take measures supporting staff and other persons acting on their behalf, taking account of technical knowledge, experience, education, training, use context, and persons/groups on whom the systems are used. | S8 | [Regulation (EU) 2026/1744](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2026/1744/oj) · amending legal act | Article 4 is replaced with the context-sensitive organizational-measures duty and the statement that providers and deployers need not guarantee any specific literacy level for any individual. |
| S3 | [UNESCO, Recommendation on the Ethics of AI (2021)](https://www.unesco.org/en/legal-affairs/recommendation-ethics-artificial-intelligence) · intergovernmental recommendation | Sections 44–47 connect public awareness and literacy to accessible education, civic engagement, human rights, participation, and multi-stakeholder governance. | S4 | [NIST AI 600-1, Generative AI Profile (2024)](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.600-1.pdf) · voluntary risk-management guidance | Over-reliance / automation bias is a risk consideration in human interaction with generative AI. NIST guidance is not evidence that all users or tasks experience the risk. |
| S5 | [Pearson et al., *Scientific Reports* (2026)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-34983-y) · bounded empirical study | N=295 participants judged whether faces were real or synthetic with guidance presented as human or AI. The study reports task-specific reliance and performance patterns; it does not establish behavior in professional or high-stakes settings generally. |
| S6 | [Hou et al., *Computers & Education* (2026)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S036013152600014X) · bounded empirical study | N=226 participants in a problem-based collaborative learning study. Its intervention reduced direct adoption of AI-generated content in that setting; it did not significantly improve self-reported critical thinking over the short intervention. This is not a general causal claim about AI literacy. |

## Verification record

- S1–S8 were opened or searched at their primary/official publication pages on 2026-10-01, including the Commission FAQ, Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, and the consolidated AI Act text. The amended Article 4 mandates no specific literacy level for any individual and directs organizations to consider people/groups on whom systems are used. The Commission FAQ says no person-by-person measurement or certificate is required and describes internal records as optional, but its opening summary retains “ensure a sufficient level” wording that conflicts with the later amendment answer. Separate FAQ answers also list different enforcement start dates. The page flags the wording mismatch and omits the dates.
- The map is a projection of source-supported and authored claims, not legal advice, a systematic literature review, or proof that the recommended practice is effective.
