# Corpus Review Process

> 04 / Portfolio reference

The corpus review is the reading test. It is the four-step process that every source passes through before it enters a claim.

## The four steps

1. **Select** — choose the source by authority and relevance. A source is a citable, original statement; it is not a summary or a paraphrase.
2. **Frame** — extract the claim, the boundary, and the question. What does the source say, what does it not say, and what question does it answer?
3. **Read** — read the full source, not a summary. The reading is complete; the review does not skip sections.
4. **Receipt** — record the source, the digest, and the review state. The receipt is a bounded process report; it does not authorize the next action.

## What it is not

The corpus review is not a rubric, a checklist, or a gate. It is a reading discipline: the source is read in full, the claim is bounded, and the review state is recorded honestly. A source that has not been reviewed is marked as such; it is not silently treated as reviewed.

## Where it fits

The corpus review is the first of two bounded processes in the ESS. The second is the implementation campaign (the action test). Together they form the reading-then-acting cycle that makes the work inspectable.
