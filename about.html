About the founder

The interface was wrong.
Not me.

I'm Scott. I build Hearth & Code, and I'm also the first person it had to work for.

I've loved computers my whole life. Not as a career thing, though it became that too, but as the place my head went when it was supposed to be somewhere else. AI and machine learning, game development, audio and signal processing, type theory, programming languages, category theory, the way systems lock together when you finally get them right. It's more than one lifetime could hold and I've mostly made my peace with that. The breadth was always the point, and if I'm honest it was also part of the trouble.

Here's the part I spent years not understanding. I could follow the ideas. I could generate them, connect them across domains nobody told me were connected, see a few moves ahead in a system that didn't exist yet. What I could not do was the middle part. The starting. The sitting down and staying down. The translation from "I understand this completely" into "here it is, finished, in your hands." I dropped two graduate programs. I have a gap in my work history I never figured out how to explain. And for a long stretch I believed the obvious reading of all that, which is that I was broken, that I'd been locked out of the thing I loved most by something I couldn't name and couldn't fix.

I want to be careful here, because this is the spot where people reach for the inspirational arc, and it wasn't one. What changed wasn't willpower. It wasn't a new system or finally wanting it badly enough. It was AI. At first, specifically, it was Claude, external cognitive infrastructure sitting right next to me, carrying the exact part of the work my brain was never going to push through on its own. And the relief wasn't really about getting more done, though I did get more done. It was quieter and stranger than that. The problem had been structural the whole time, not personal. The capability was there all along. The interface was just wrong.

That last one, the interface was wrong and not me, is the reason any of this exists.

I have ADHD and I'm bipolar. I've stopped framing those as things to apologize for or quietly hide in the corner. The hyperfocus, the six threads at once, the pattern-sense that won't switch off, none of that was ever the deficit. The deficit was a world built for a different nervous system, handing me tools that assumed I worked the way it did. So I'm building the thing I needed. A place to learn the foundations and the real use of these tools, so you can go explore whatever you're actually curious about, that meets you differently on a low day than a high day, because they genuinely are not the same day, and almost nothing I've ever used acted like it knew that, which honestly might be most of the problem right there if I had to compress it.

Now the harder part to write. Am I sure about all of this? Honestly, no. I carry a real confidence and a real doubt at the same time, usually in the same hour, and I've decided that's allowed, maybe even the honest way to do it. I've shipped production software for over a decade and I still get things wrong constantly. I would rather show you the rough version than a clean one that's lying to you. But the center of it I'm sure about, and it's small enough to fit in a line.

"If the tools we build don't actually help you grow, then they're wrong, and we revise them."

You're not building alone. I wasn't, in the end, and that's really the only thing I'm trying to say here.
